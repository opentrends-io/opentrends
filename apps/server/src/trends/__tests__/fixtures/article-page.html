Harbor city pilots quiet electric ferries

By Dana Reyes · September 27, 2026

The harbor authority replaced two diesel ferries with battery-powered boats this week, starting a six-month trial that officials say will decide whether the whole fleet switches by 2029.

Each boat carries up to 150 passengers and charges for about twelve minutes at either end of the crossing. Operators say the vessels are quieter on deck & noticeably smoother in rough water, and that the charging stops fit inside the existing timetable.

What riders will notice

Commuters will see a new boarding lane and a live display showing the battery level. Ticket prices and departure times stay the same during the trial.

  • Crossings still leave every twenty minutes at peak hours.

  • Bikes ride free on the lower deck.

  • Night service keeps the older diesel boat for now.

“If the batteries hold up through winter, we won’t buy another diesel hull,” the authority’s fleet manager said.

The open questions

Engineers want to know how cold weather affects range and how quickly the shore chargers wear out. The authority will publish monthly reliability figures, including missed sailings and average charge times, on its website.

Neighbouring ports are watching closely. Two of them have already asked to borrow the charging design if the pilot succeeds.
